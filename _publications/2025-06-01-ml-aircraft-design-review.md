This article reviews recent research on the use of machine learning in aircraft design. It covers how methods such as deep learning and reinforcement learning are applied to complex, nonlinear design problems across aerodynamics and fluid dynamics, acoustics, structural analysis and health monitoring, and automatic control, and it compares techniques such as CNNs, RNN/LSTM models, reinforcement learning and random forests along with their limitations.

A recurring theme is that machine learning can cut the computational cost of design studies while maintaining or improving the accuracy of simulations, which shortens the design cycle. The review concludes that data-driven methods are contributing across every area of aircraft design surveyed, and that their continued development is likely to reshape how modern aircraft are designed.
